**1. The BlueZ test can report success when the required behaviour is broken.**

In [`reconnect_check()`](https://github.com/ivoitovych/qca9377-bt-hang/blob/4c82e91900f0599e6a6c4310d5eefa996c78f344/contributions/CO-02-sco-connect-reuses-closing-link/patch/bluez/0001-tools-sco-tester-Add-reconnect-tests.patch), any immediate error other than `EAGAIN` or `EINPROGRESS` becomes a pass.

That applies to **both** test cases.

Consequently:

- “Reconnect After Disconnect” passes if the second connection immediately fails, despite its stated requirement that it obtain a working link.
- “Reconnect During Disconnect” accepts unrelated failures such as `EINVAL`, `ENOMEM`, or `ENETDOWN` as evidence of correct handling.

This is a concrete defect in the pass criteria, not merely a preference about test style.

Require successful reconnection in the After case. For the During case, accept the specifically intended refusal—currently `ECONNABORTED`—or verify successful deferred establishment. Unexpected errors must fail.

After changing it, deliberately inject an unexpected error and confirm that the test fails. That checks the assertion itself, rather than merely repeating successful runs.

**2. The emulator does not actually complete the held disconnection.**

The test hooks `HCIEMU_HOOK_PRE_EVT` for the Disconnect command and returns `false`. In the reviewed [emulator implementation](https://github.com/bluez/bluez/blob/7bc9684dc9e4/emulator/btdev.c), that skips `cmd_disconnect_complete()` altogether.

That function normally removes the emulator connection and performs peer-side cleanup. Later, `reconnect_release()` calls `btdev_send_event()`, which only emits the event; it does not perform that cleanup.

Thus the test tells the kernel that the old connection has disappeared while retaining it inside the emulator.

This does **not** invalidate the observed kernel reuse failure: withholding the completion exposes that failure. However, it weakens the claim that the same test validates correct recovery and a fresh connection under realistic controller state.

Prefer intercepting and delaying delivery of the completion event while allowing normal emulator cleanup, or explicitly completing the delayed operation through a suitable emulator helper. Verify that the old connection is removed before declaring the replacement successful.

**3. Patch 1 does not establish synchronization with an already-running timeout worker.**

The important source relationships are:

- `sco_connect()` holds `hdev->lock`.
- `hci_conn_timeout()` reads `refcnt` and calls `hci_abort_conn()` without acquiring that lock.
- `hci_abort_conn()` writes `abort_reason`.
- `hci_conn_hold()` uses `cancel_delayed_work()`, which does not wait for an already-running worker.

Therefore the following interleaving remains possible from the reviewed code:

1. The timeout worker observes zero holds.
2. The new connect sees `abort_reason == 0`.
3. The new connect takes its hold and attaches to the existing connection.
4. The timeout worker proceeds with the abort.

**This is a source-derived residual race, not a failure I reproduced, and not proof that your patch introduces a regression.** It does mean that neither the device lock nor the reported KCSAN runs establishes complete protection.

Your submitted reproducer starts the second connect after the controller has received Disconnect. It intentionally tests a later window, when the abort marker is already set.

Before claiming complete handling, either establish the missing synchronization argument or add a targeted experiment around the worker’s zero-count decision and subsequent abort. If this remains a separate pre-existing race, explicitly scope patch 1 to connections already marked aborting. Do not casually replace cancellation with synchronous cancellation under the device lock; that needs a deadlock analysis.

**4. Patch 2’s ownership correction looks right, and its revised `Fixes:` tag is defensible.**

The source supports the explanation:

- `hci_connect_acl()` supplies an ACL hold.
- `hci_conn_link()` takes a SCO hold when creating a link.
- Reusing an existing link takes no additional SCO hold.
- The current `sco_connect()` releases its caller-owned hold on its relevant success and failure paths.

Taking the missing SCO hold and releasing the redundant ACL hold restores the expected accounting on reuse.

There is also a particularly close accepted precedent: [`e06d549fcd4a`, “Bluetooth: hci_conn: fix CIS hold ownership on reuse”](https://github.com/bluez/bluetooth-next/commit/e06d549fcd4a). It explicitly handles the existing-link case and drops the redundant parent hold.

**5. Patch 2’s regression test should be available to reviewers.**

The submitted BlueZ patch contains the During and After cases. The decisive “Connect Twice” and “Connect Twice ACL” cases exist only as reported experiments referencing a local experimental binary.

For a strong submission, make the essential patch-2 reproducer available—preferably as an additional `sco-tester` case. Its assertions should show:

- The second socket receives `EBUSY`.
- The first socket remains usable.
- The rejected attempt does not trigger disconnection of the first link.
- Closing the owner releases the connection normally.

This is a better investment than adding more repetitions of the patch-1 test. It gives maintainers a durable regression check for the second fix.

**6. The testing summary needs qualification.**

The [cover letter](https://github.com/ivoitovych/qca9377-bt-hang/blob/4c82e91900f0599e6a6c4310d5eefa996c78f344/contributions/CO-02-sco-connect-reuses-closing-link/drafts/kernel-cover-letter.txt) says other tester results are unchanged. The dossier also records management-test flakes, including differing counts in later pre-send checks.

That does not establish a regression caused by your patches. It also does not support an unqualified statement of identical results.

Keep the mail concise, but distinguish:

- The reproducible SCO failure and its corrected outcome.
- Other suites’ results.
- Management-test instability observed with both configurations.
- The absence of hardware validation.

Likewise, “42/42 failure paths including races” in the log is a report of testing, not independently inspectable evidence when the cases and results remain in `tmp/co02-send/`.

You do not need to publish hundreds of logs. A small committed manifest of cases, expected outcomes, commands, and representative results would make this substantially easier to audit.

**7. Some packaging and written-policy issues remain.**

These are smaller than the test defects, but worth fixing:

- The dossier’s top-level README still recommends `patch/kernel/`, which contains superseded messages and the old tag.
- The results narrative still names the superseded patch-2 `Fixes:` tag. Preserve historical records, but add a conspicuous correction and link to the final patches.
- The cover subject remains long despite shortening the individual patch subjects.
- Several added multiline comments in the BlueZ test do not follow its documented M2 convention, which starts the text on the line after `/*`.
- Kernel signoffs are present, and the BlueZ patch correctly omits them. Those projects deliberately have different rules. See [BlueZ’s maintainer guidelines](https://github.com/bluez/bluez/blob/master/doc/maintainer-guidelines.rst).

The related SCO discussion provides a concrete precedent for developing BlueZ tests alongside kernel fixes; it does not justify silently introducing expected CI failures. **Refusal is a reasonable first contribution, but it does not fix the complete PipeWire symptom.**

Your experiments are commendably explicit about this: immediate retries still occur before teardown completes. Refusal corrects the misleading success result and gives userspace an actionable error. It does not make existing PipeWire 1.0.5 recover.

Keep that limitation prominent. The deferral prototype introduces substantially more lifecycle machinery—queued work, socket references, cancellation, controller shutdown, and completion notification. It needs its own review. Also, its assumption that observing `abort_reason` means the abort is already queued needs examination: the marker is set before queueing.

**Does this follow successful contribution practice?**

In several important ways, yes:

- Two focused changes, with user-visible consequences.
- An explicit base commit.
- Historical explanation rather than an unexplained reference-count adjustment.
- A controlled reproduction and a control case.
- Compatibility testing with neighbouring work.
- A clear admission that the minimal fix does not solve the whole userspace problem.

These align with the kernel’s expectations for self-contained explanations, separate logical changes, and useful regression history. [The Linux Kernel documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/process/submitting-patches.html)

The accepted CIS ownership fix is a strong positive comparison. The nearby SCO series also supplies a useful caution: Luiz requested broader correctness treatment and later flagged additional problems despite extensive testing. That supports prioritizing precise state and ownership reasoning over impressive test totals. It would be inaccurate to describe those revision requests as definitive rejection of the contribution. [lkml.rescloud.iu.edu](https://lkml.rescloud.iu.edu/2610.0/13021.html)

I examined concrete precedents, not a statistically representative collection of accepted and rejected submissions, so I would not claim a predicted acceptance probability.

**The ELL installation and permission cleanups are separate local matters.** Keep them out of upstream mail. A private build prefix is preferable for future ELL builds; removal should use a verified installation inventory. The reviewed dossier does not define permission cleanups A and B sufficiently for me to assess them.

My send gate would be: **correct the test assertions and emulator teardown, make patch 2’s reproducer available, resolve or explicitly delimit the timeout race, and reconcile the submission text.** Those are targeted improvements. Once addressed, this would give maintainers a small, understandable fix with evidence they can use, rather than asking them to reconstruct the investigation.
