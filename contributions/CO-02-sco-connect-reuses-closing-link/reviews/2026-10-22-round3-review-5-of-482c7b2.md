## Reviewer 5

**I found no new blocking defect in v2’s patch 3 or the revised BlueZ test. I would send this version for upstream review.** That is a readiness judgment—not a guarantee of acceptance or proof that every possible race is eliminated.

I reviewed the pinned package at `482c7b2`, its source context, and the recorded evidence. I did **not** rerun the kernels or independently inspect the 32 raw logs, which the evidence document locates on your development host.

1. **Patch 3 now covers the missing incoming-connection case.**

   Moving the synchronous cancellation after both branches is the right correction. Both an already-linked connection and an incoming connection that acquires its link here now take a hold, wait for the disconnect worker, and recheck `abort_reason`.

   The refusal path appears balanced:

   | Path | Ownership on refusal |
   |---|---|
   | Existing link | The extra ACL hold has already been dropped; the new SCO hold is dropped. |
   | Newly created link | The SCO hold is dropped; the link retains the ACL hold until `hci_conn_unlink()` releases it. |

   The wait’s safety is supported by the SCO-specific worker call chain: the worker does not need the held `hdev->lock` to finish. The documented parked-worker runs exercise precisely that blocking situation.

   **My earlier readiness assessment missed the incoming, unlinked path.** V2 fixes a substantive gap, and the counterexample against v1 is valuable evidence.

2. **The BlueZ test now has substantially better protection against false passes.**

   The revised checks address the important weaknesses:

   - Both connection callbacks reject error and hang-up readiness.
   - Successful readiness also requires appropriate poll results and no socket error.
   - The replacement connection must have its own setup/completion sequence and remain connected after the quiet interval.
   - Connect Twice checks that the first socket survives the rejected second connection.
   - ACL teardown is identified by the recorded ACL handle.

   Allowing either immediate refusal or a genuinely new connection in the During case remains reasonable: the test checks correct behaviour without prescribing one future kernel implementation.

   These three upstream tests do **not** deterministically exercise patch 3’s worker race. The separate parked-worker experiments supply that evidence. Keep that distinction explicit; it is not a reason to put intrusive kernel instrumentation into the submitted test.

3. **The evidence supports submission, with two wording qualifications.**

   The repeated base/v2 management-test comparisons are useful: matching failing cases are much more informative than matching totals. They support **“no additional failures observed; the extra timeout also reproduces on the unpatched base.”** That is more precise than categorically proving the series cannot influence that timeout.

   Also, the [evidence document](https://github.com/ivoitovych/qca9377-bt-hang/blob/482c7b250f2af35fa116aa12c64e977ce1519770/contributions/CO-02-sco-connect-reuses-closing-link/experiments/results-2026-10-22-v2.md) describes **local CI checks**, with `scan-build` unavailable and `VerifyFixes` checked manually. Say “all executed checks passed,” rather than implying every upstream CI check ran. These are reporting refinements, not code blockers.

4. **The `READ_ONCE` explanation should remain modest.**

   “Other accesses are plain” establishes existing practice; it does not establish synchronization correctness. The important argument here is the completed synchronous cancellation followed by the second check.

   I would not request a speculative `READ_ONCE` change as a prerequisite to sending this patch. Nor would I present the tree-wide convention as proof that no data race exists.

   Keep the email self-contained and the dossier optional. Maintainers should not need to read the review history to understand the fix. A link to a compact, sanitized parked-race transcript would improve external auditability, but I would not delay submission for another broad testing round.
