1. Replace "The series has not yet been run on hardware; the bug was found on a QCA9377 laptop
   with PipeWire 1.0.5." with:

It was also run on the QCA9377 laptop where the bug was found, with the
Bluetooth module of Ubuntu 24.04's 7.0.0-38 kernel rebuilt with
abd93c85c8667, 816fb1590a4c and this series (see the last paragraph),
and PipeWire 1.0.5, through more than a day and a half of ordinary use
with three headsets. On the earbuds whose AT+BCS reply comes before
the old link's Disconnection Complete, connect() failed with
ECONNABORTED at each of three codec changes instead of succeeding
without a link. On the two headsets that reply after it, each of eight
codec changes got a new link. All 36 SCO links of that boot ended with
a successful Disconnection Complete for their handle, no command timed
out, and the kernel log has no WARNING, BUG, refcount or use-after-free
report; its "SCO packet for unknown connection handle" and "corrupted
SCO packet" errors also occur without this series. Patch 1's case, a
second connect while the first socket's link is up, and patch 3's race
were not exercised on the laptop; both are covered by the emulator
tests above, the race with the debug change that widens its window.

2. Replace the paragraph "The series does not by itself make PipeWire's codec switch succeed:
   ..." with:

The series does not by itself make PipeWire's codec change seamless.
PipeWire 1.0.5 retries a connect that fails with ECONNABORTED at once,
while the old link is still being disconnected, so its retries fail
too. On the laptop, with a stream playing, the audio came back about 5
seconds later, when WirePlumber suspended the failed nodes and the next
connect got a new link. In an earlier reproduction without the series
(7.0.0-34), the same codec change left the socket connected without a
link and no link followed at all: sounds started 3 and 26 seconds later
were still silent. A prototype that instead defers such a connect until
the old link has been torn down lets the retry succeed in the emulator
tests (it has not been run on hardware); it builds on this series and
changes connect() semantics, so I can post it as an RFC if that approach
is preferred.
