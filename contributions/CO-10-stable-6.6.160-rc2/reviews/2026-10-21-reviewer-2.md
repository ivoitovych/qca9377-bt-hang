### The strongest part: the experimental design

The work in `experiments/results-2026-10-21.md` is unusually good for this kind of reply.

You did not merely build rc2 and say “works for me.” You built **the released control, 6.6.159, and the candidate, 6.6.160-rc2**, with the same BlueZ tester configuration and on the same QEMU/KVM environment. You then demonstrated an expected delta rather than merely an absence of crashes.

For BT-9, the meaningful race case is **bad on the control 3/3 and good on rc2 3/3**, while the normal-order control passes on both. That is excellent causal evidence.

For BT-8, the dedicated `hci_vhci` reproducer gives **the erroneous Success status on 6.6.159 and Not Powered on rc2**, twice each. Again, this is not “it seems fixed”; it directly observes the semantic behavior the patch changes.

Then the full `mgmt-tester` comparison provides a particularly good sanity check: 508 cases, with exactly one verdict improvement corresponding to the intended BT-9 fix and no newly worse case. This is much stronger evidence than simply running the test suite and reporting its aggregate pass rate.

That approach matches what makes useful negative stable-review reports good as well: real stable regression reports give the exact failing configuration, distinguish new regressions, establish reproducibility, and identify the failure rather than making a vague claim. [Openwall Lists](https://lists.openwall.net/linux-kernel/2025/06/23/1823)

## What I would change before sending

1. **Do not send while `[PENDING]` remains.** Finish the other BlueZ testers. For every pair, verify not only that the script finished but that both logs contain a complete `Total:` result and that the **per-test verdict comparison** is identical. Your runner scripts intentionally do not stop on tester exit status, so script completion by itself is insufficient. If any verdict differs, rerun that particular tester/case several times before deciding whether it is a regression or flake.

2. **Replace “No regressions in Bluetooth”.** This is the one materially overbroad sentence. QEMU BlueZ emulator testers exercise a substantial Bluetooth surface, but they do not establish the absence of every Bluetooth regression across real controllers, drivers and architectures. Use something like **“No new failures in the BlueZ emulator tests I ran:”**. That says precisely what you proved.

3. **Put the exact rc2 commit in the mail.** I would say `6.6.160-rc2 (c578c158a368)`. This is unusually valuable for `linux-stable-rc`, because the official stable documentation explicitly warns that the stable-rc tree changes frequently and is rebased. [Linux Kernel Archives](https://cdn.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/process/stable-kernel-rules.html) Your dossier already records the full SHA; exposing the short SHA costs almost nothing and removes ambiguity permanently.

4. **Expose the existing `mgmt-tester` failures rather than accidentally sounding like 508/508 passed.** A very strong compact formulation would be: `mgmt-tester: 465 pass / 37 fail / 6 time out on rc2 versus 464 / 38 / 6 on 6.6.159; the only verdict change is ...`. Existing failures are not a problem because your differential proves they are pre-existing. Giving the totals makes that crystal clear.

5. **Perform two cheap sanity checks before the mail is frozen:** verify the saved configs actually contain `CONFIG_KASAN=y`, `CONFIG_PROVE_LOCKING=y` and the expected debugging options on both builds, and verify the guest boot logs identify the intended 6.6.159 and 6.6.160-rc2 kernels. Your scripts preserve enough information for this; it is simply worth making the last audit explicit.

6. **One tiny repository cleanup:** `all-testers.sh` says verdicts will be compared with `compare-verdicts.py`, while the actual tool is `scripts/compare-tester-verdicts.py`. This has zero effect on the upstream contribution, but if the target is immaculate internal documentation, I would fix the comment.

### The QCA9377 patch

I recommend **leaving the untested QCA9377 entry out of the upstream mail**, exactly as the current draft does.

This is not dishonest. A stable-review `Tested-by` is not a certification that the tester personally exercised every patch in a 1,651-patch release candidate. Successful testers routinely test only a particular architecture, configuration, machine, or QEMU environment. The official process explicitly permits this scoped testing. [cdn.kernel.org](https://cdn.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/process/stable-kernel-rules.html)

And your mail does not claim to have hardware-tested the QCA9377 entry. It says exactly what was exercised.

The dossier, meanwhile, correctly documents that the QCA9377 device-table change was **not tested on rc2 hardware**. That is where the qualification belongs.

A real-hardware rc2 boot on the QCA9377 laptop would certainly increase coverage, especially because that device-ID addition is one of the changes associated with this project. But I would classify it as **valuable optional coverage, not missing homework and not a prerequisite for the `Tested-by`**. I would not reboot the machine solely because you think stable etiquette demands it. It does not.

This is particularly true for a device-ID addition: stable policy explicitly recognizes adding device IDs as an appropriate stable change. [Linux Kernel Archives](https://cdn.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/process/stable-kernel-rules.html)

## On “rejected contributions”

There is one conceptual correction to your requested comparison: there isn't really a meaningful category of **“rejected `Tested-by` stable-review contributions”** analogous to rejected patches. This isn't a patch asking for inclusion. It is test evidence offered during an explicitly solicited review period.

The bad patterns to avoid are therefore different: an unscoped “everything works” claim after narrow testing; reporting only totals while hiding new failures; confusing pre-existing failures with regressions; reporting a one-off flaky result as deterministic; testing a moving `stable-rc` tree without identifying the exact snapshot; or making the maintainer follow external links simply to understand what was tested.

Your work avoids virtually all of those already. The one place it currently brushes against that bad pattern is the sentence **“No regressions in Bluetooth”**. Fixing those four words matters disproportionately.
